Q: How is the periodic (monthly) interest rate derived from the annual nominal rate — simple division (annual rate / 12), or an effective monthly rate compounded from the annual figure? And what day-count/accrual convention applies (e.g. 30/360, actual/365, or a flat monthly period regardless of days)?
A: Simple division: monthly rate = annual nominal rate / 12. Day-count convention is 30/360 — every month is treated as 30 days, the year as 360 — so dividing by 12 is exact under that convention. Interest is computed once per monthly period; there is no daily accrual.

Q: Rounding policy: to what precision are interest and principal amounts rounded (e.g. 2 decimal places / currency minor units), what rounding mode (half-up, half-even, truncate), and where does any accumulated rounding residual go — adjusted into each instalment or absorbed entirely by the final instalment so the closing balance is exactly zero?
A: Amounts are in KES. Instalments are rounded to the whole shilling (no cents). Within each period, interest is computed and rounded first; principal is the remainder of the rounded instalment. Rounding mode is half-up [default, not policy]. All accumulated rounding residuals are absorbed entirely by the final instalment so the closing balance is exactly zero.

Q: Payment dates: when is the first instalment due relative to the disbursement date (e.g. exactly one month after), and how are subsequent due dates generated — fixed day-of-month with month-end roll handling (e.g. 31st in a 30-day month), business-day adjustment, or simple calendar-month increments?
A: The first instalment is due exactly one calendar month after the disbursement date; subsequent due dates are generated by successive calendar-month increments from that date. No business-day adjustment or day-of-month roll rule has been specified [default, not policy: simple calendar-month increment, no adjustment].

Q: The amortisation method is straight-line (fixed principal P/n per period), yet the rounding answer described principal as 'the remainder of the rounded instalment', which reads like an annuity where a fixed total is rounded and principal is backed out. For straight-line, which holds: (a) principal each period = round(P/n) fixed, with interest rounded separately and the total simply being their sum (total varies, is never itself rounded); or (b) a target total instalment is rounded and principal = rounded total minus rounded interest?
A: (b): within each regular period, compute the natural instalment total (P/n + interest on the opening balance), round that total to the whole shilling, then round interest separately, and take principal as the remainder (rounded instalment minus rounded interest). Principal is therefore not independently fixed at round(P/n) each period — it absorbs the within-period rounding residual. P/n describes the amortisation structure; rounding is applied to the total, not to principal in isolation.

A: Yes: the final instalment's principal is whatever outstanding balance remains (not the standard P/n for that period), clearing it to exactly zero; interest for the final period is computed normally on that period's opening balance and rounded in the usual way.

## Produced specification

# Requirements Document

## Introduction

This feature is the repayment schedule engine for our loan product. Given a disbursed loan, defined by a principal amount, an annual nominal interest rate, a term expressed as a whole number of monthly instalments, and a disbursement date, the engine produces the full repayment schedule. For each instalment the schedule reports the interest due, the principal due, and the running outstanding balance after the instalment is applied.

The public interface is a single function, `generate_schedule(loan)`, which accepts the loan and returns the ordered schedule of instalments.

The institution amortises on a straight-line (equal principal) basis: each period carries a fixed principal portion of P/n with interest charged on the reducing balance, so the total instalment declines over the term. Interest is derived by simple division of the annual nominal rate by twelve under a 30/360 day-count convention. Amounts are denominated in Kenyan shillings (KES) and rounded to the whole shilling. Interest accrues from the disbursement date, so where disbursement precedes the nominal start of the first period a pro-rated stub charge is levied for the extra days. Accumulated rounding residuals are absorbed by the final instalment so the closing balance is exactly zero.

Throughout this document, items marked **[default]** were not fixed by the stakeholder and are resolved by the authoring process rather than by institutional policy. All other requirements reflect explicit stakeholder decisions.

## Requirements

### Requirement 1: Schedule generation and output shape

**User Story:** As a loan servicing engineer, I want `generate_schedule(loan)` to return the complete ordered repayment schedule, so that every instalment's interest, principal, and running balance can be presented to borrowers and posted to ledgers.

#### Acceptance Criteria

1. WHEN `generate_schedule(loan)` is called with a loan comprising a principal, an annual nominal interest rate, a term in monthly instalments, and a disbursement date THEN the system SHALL return a schedule containing exactly one entry per instalment in the term, ordered from first to last.
2. WHEN the schedule is produced THEN each instalment entry SHALL carry its due date, the interest due, the principal due, and the outstanding balance remaining after that instalment is applied.
3. WHEN an instalment's principal is applied THEN the system SHALL compute its running balance as the prior period's closing balance minus that instalment's principal portion.
4. WHEN the final instalment is applied THEN the system SHALL report a running balance of exactly zero.
5. WHERE the term is n monthly instalments THEN the system SHALL produce n instalment entries and no more.

### Requirement 2: Straight-line (equal principal) amortisation

**User Story:** As a product owner, I want each instalment to retire a fixed share of principal with interest on the reducing balance, so that repayments follow the straight-line method the institution has chosen rather than a fixed-total annuity.

#### Acceptance Criteria

1. WHEN computing the amortisation structure for a loan of principal P over n instalments THEN the system SHALL use a nominal fixed principal portion of P/n per period as the basis for each regular instalment.
2. WHEN computing each period's interest THEN the system SHALL charge interest on the opening outstanding balance of that period, which reduces as principal is retired.
3. WHEN successive instalments are compared THEN the system SHALL produce declining total instalment amounts over the term, because the principal portion is broadly level while the interest portion falls.
4. The system SHALL NOT use annuity / EMI (equal total payment) amortisation.

### Requirement 3: Periodic interest rate and day-count convention

**User Story:** As a finance controller, I want the monthly interest rate derived by simple division under a 30/360 convention, so that interest charges match our stated accrual basis exactly.

#### Acceptance Criteria

1. WHEN deriving the periodic rate from the annual nominal rate THEN the system SHALL compute the monthly rate as the annual nominal rate divided by twelve.
2. WHEN accruing interest for a regular monthly period THEN the system SHALL apply the 30/360 day-count convention, treating every month as 30 days and the year as 360 days.
3. WHEN interest is accrued THEN the system SHALL compute it once per monthly period and SHALL NOT perform daily accrual within a regular period.
4. The system SHALL NOT derive an effective monthly rate by compounding the annual figure.

### Requirement 4: Currency, rounding precision, and rounding mode

**User Story:** As a ledger integrator, I want all amounts in whole KES using a consistent rounding rule, so that posted figures reconcile to the shilling with no fractional residue.

#### Acceptance Criteria

1. WHEN amounts are reported THEN the system SHALL denominate them in Kenyan shillings (KES) and SHALL round them to the whole shilling, carrying no cents or minor units.
2. WHEN rounding any amount THEN the system SHALL use half-up rounding. **[default]**
3. WHEN computing a regular instalment THEN the system SHALL first compute the natural instalment total for the period as the P/n principal portion plus the interest on the period's opening balance, and SHALL round that total to the whole shilling.
4. WHEN the rounded instalment total has been determined THEN the system SHALL round the period's interest separately to the whole shilling.
5. WHEN both the rounded total and the rounded interest are known THEN the system SHALL set the period's principal as the rounded instalment total minus the rounded interest, so that principal absorbs the within-period rounding residual.
6. The system SHALL NOT fix each period's principal independently at round(P/n); P/n describes the amortisation structure, while rounding is applied to the total rather than to principal in isolation.

### Requirement 5: Rounding residual and zero closing balance

**User Story:** As a finance controller, I want all accumulated rounding differences cleared by the last instalment, so that the loan closes to exactly zero without manual adjustment.

#### Acceptance Criteria

1. WHEN the schedule is generated THEN the system SHALL absorb all accumulated rounding residuals entirely into the final instalment.
2. WHEN computing the final instalment's principal THEN the system SHALL set it to the entire outstanding balance at the start of that period, clearing the balance to exactly zero, rather than to the standard P/n portion.
3. WHEN computing the final instalment's interest THEN the system SHALL compute it normally on that period's opening balance and round it in the usual way to the whole shilling.
4. WHEN the final instalment is applied THEN the closing balance SHALL be exactly zero.

### Requirement 6: Payment due dates

**User Story:** As a collections scheduler, I want due dates generated by calendar-month increments from disbursement, so that instalment dates are predictable and anchored to the disbursement date.

#### Acceptance Criteria

1. WHEN the first instalment's due date is determined THEN the system SHALL set it to exactly one calendar month after the disbursement date.
2. WHEN subsequent due dates are determined THEN the system SHALL generate them by successive calendar-month increments from the first due date.
3. The system SHALL NOT apply any business-day adjustment to due dates. **[default]**
4. The system SHALL NOT apply any day-of-month roll rule for month-end cases beyond the simple calendar-month increment. **[default]**

### Requirement 7: Interest accrual from disbursement and first-period stub

**User Story:** As a finance controller, I want interest to accrue from the actual disbursement date, so that the borrower is charged for every day funds are outstanding, including any days before the nominal first period begins.

#### Acceptance Criteria

1. WHEN interest begins to accrue THEN the system SHALL accrue it from the disbursement date.
2. IF the disbursement date falls before the nominal start of the first period THEN the system SHALL levy a pro-rated stub interest charge for those extra days.
3. WHEN the first period is charged THEN the system SHALL allow a pro-rated partial-period interest charge and SHALL NOT force the first period to a full standard period.
4. WHEN computing the stub interest THEN the system SHALL compute it as (annual rate / 12) × (actual extra days / 30) × the opening principal. **[default]**

### Requirement 8: Presentation of the first instalment

**User Story:** As a borrower reading my schedule, I want the first instalment to show a single interest figure, so that the schedule reads consistently even when a stub charge applies.

#### Acceptance Criteria

1. WHEN a stub interest charge applies THEN the system SHALL fold it into the first instalment's interest figure rather than reporting it as a separate line or charge. **[default]**
2. WHEN the first instalment is computed THEN its interest figure SHALL combine the full first-period interest and the stub interest into a single interest amount. **[default]**
3. WHEN the first instalment is computed THEN it SHALL carry the standard P/n principal portion as its amortisation basis, subject to the rounding rules in Requirement 4. **[default]**

### Requirement 9: Input assumptions and edge handling

**User Story:** As an engineer consuming the engine, I want the loan inputs and basic edge cases handled predictably, so that the schedule is well defined across the supported range of loans.

#### Acceptance Criteria

1. WHEN a loan is supplied THEN the system SHALL read its principal, annual nominal interest rate, term in whole monthly instalments, and disbursement date as the sole inputs to the computation.
2. WHEN the disbursement date coincides with the nominal first-period start such that there are no extra days THEN the system SHALL levy no stub interest and SHALL treat the first period as a standard full period.
3. WHEN the term is a single instalment (n = 1) THEN the system SHALL produce one instalment whose principal clears the full outstanding balance to zero and whose interest is computed on the opening balance, consistent with Requirement 5. **[default]**
4. WHEN rounding residuals accumulate across periods THEN no residual SHALL remain unallocated after the final instalment is applied.
