## The requirements don't hold together. I'm not shipping until one of them changes.

Before any code, the arithmetic:

- Stated rule: fee = 3% × instalment = 0.03 × 500 = **15**.
- Stated band: 10 ≤ fee ≤ 12.
- 15 is not in [10, 12].

All three "confirmed" facts cannot be true at once. Confirmed doesn't mean consistent, and the specific clash is between the rate (3% at an instalment of 500) and the ceiling (12). This isn't a rounding artefact or a decimal-literal issue: 3% of 500 is exactly 15, three whole units above the cap.

For the 3% rule and the 10–12 band to coexist, one of the three numbers has to move:

- **Rate is wrong.** To land in [10, 12] at an instalment of 500, the rate has to be 2%–2.4%.
- **Instalment is wrong.** For 3% to land in [10, 12], the instalment has to be 333.33–400, not 500.
- **Band is wrong.** If 3% × 500 = 15 is the intended fee, the band has to include 15.

## The decision I will *not* make silently

There are two readings of "must land between 10 and 12 inclusive", and they produce completely different systems. A naive implementation picks one and buries the conflict. I won't.

**Reading A — it's an invariant.** The fee produced by the rules is expected to fall in that range naturally, and the band is a validation guard. Under this reading the given inputs are simply contradictory and there is nothing correct to build. Stop.

**Reading B — it's a clamp** (min 10, max 12 applied after the percentage). Then fee = min(max(0.03 × amount, 10), 12). At an instalment of 500 this is `min(max(15, 10), 12)` = **12 for every instalment**, and the 3% rule never actually bites, it's dead weight documentation. The band only starts doing interesting work below an instalment of ~400 (floor at 333.33) and the 3% only matters between 333.33 and 400. If B is intended, the "3% of 500" framing is actively misleading and should be dropped or re-scoped, because at the quoted instalment the percentage is irrelevant.

I can't tell A from B from what you've given me, and the choice isn't mine to invent. It's the difference between "these requirements are broken, fix them" and "ship a clamp where the headline rule is inert".
