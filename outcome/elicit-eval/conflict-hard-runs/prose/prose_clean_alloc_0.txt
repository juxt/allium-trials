## Specification: repayment allocation

Every repayment received against a loan is divided into five buckets in fixed proportions:

- Principal — 40%
- Interest — 30%
- Fees — 20%
- Escrow — 7%
- Insurance — 3%

The proportions are fixed and sum to the whole payment, so nothing is left unallocated and nothing is counted twice. A £200 repayment yields £80 principal, £60 interest, £40 fees, £14 escrow, £6 insurance.

One domain concern I want confirmed before building. A fixed-percentage split ignores what is actually owed in each bucket. Standard repayment waterfalls allocate by priority and cap each bucket at its outstanding balance, cascading any surplus onward. A fixed split will keep sending 40% to principal after the loan is nearly repaid, and 20% to fees when no fees are outstanding. Some jurisdictions do mandate a fixed split, so this may be deliberate, but it changes the model substantially and I'd rather confirm it than assume.

Open, and needed before this is complete: the rounding rule (which bucket absorbs the sub-penny remainder so the parts still sum to the exact payment), and the late-fee behaviour itself (trigger, size, cap, grace).

## Implementation outline

Represent money as integer minor units (pence) throughout, not floats, so the parts sum to the payment exactly with no drift.

A pure `allocate(amount)` function returns the five bucket amounts. Compute four buckets by truncated integer arithmetic, then assign the residual to one nominated bucket (largest-remainder is the safe default) so the five parts always sum to `amount` exactly. Keep the percentages as named configuration rather than inline literals, so the fixed-split decision stays visible and adjustable.

Persist an allocation record per repayment (payment id plus the five amounts) for audit and reconciliation.

Property test the invariant directly: for any non-negative integer amount, the five parts are each non-negative and sum to the amount. Add worked examples (£200, and an amount that doesn't divide cleanly, e.g. £100.03) as regression cases for the rounding rule once you've chosen it.
