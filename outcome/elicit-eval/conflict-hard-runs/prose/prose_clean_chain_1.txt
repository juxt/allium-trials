The four statements are consistent: the base at 2% and the surcharge at 1% sum to the stated 3% total. So there is nothing to reconcile here, and the specification can treat the total as a derived quantity rather than an independent input.

## Intended behaviour

When a payment becomes overdue, the system charges a late fee calculated from the overdue amount. The fee has two components. The base charge is 2% of the overdue amount. The surcharge is 1% of the overdue amount. The total late fee is the sum of the two, which comes to 3% of the overdue amount.

Because the components are defined as fractions of the same overdue amount, the total is always exactly their sum. This is worth stating as an invariant rather than a separate rule: the fee is `base + surcharge`, and the "3% of the overdue amount" figure is a consequence of the two rates, not an independent constraint that could drift out of step with them. If a future change alters either rate, the total should follow automatically, so the total must never be hard-coded as a third rate.

A few boundaries the operator answers leave open, which I would confirm before building but will assume sensible defaults for:

- **Rounding.** Percentages of arbitrary amounts produce fractional currency. I would round each component to the currency's minor unit, or compute the total first and round once. The two approaches can disagree by a minor unit, so the choice needs to be pinned down. My default is to round the total once, keeping the fee exactly 3% before rounding.
- **Zero or negative overdue amounts.** A non-positive overdue amount should yield a zero fee, not a negative charge.
- **What counts as the overdue amount.** I assume it is the outstanding principal at the point the fee is assessed, excluding any previously applied late fees, so fees do not compound unless that is explicitly wanted.

## Implementation outline

I would model the fee as a single function of the overdue amount that returns the base, the surcharge and the total, so callers see the breakdown rather than an opaque figure.

- Define the two rates as named constants, `BASE_RATE = 0.02` and `SURCHARGE_RATE = 0.01`. Do not define a total-rate constant; derive the total from the components.
- Compute `base = round(amount * BASE_RATE)` and `surcharge = round(amount * SURCHARGE_RATE)`, or compute both unrounded and round the total once, per the rounding decision above. Use a decimal or integer-minor-unit representation for money rather than binary floating point, to avoid representation error on the percentages.
- Guard the input: return a zero fee for a non-positive overdue amount.
- Return a small structured result carrying `base`, `surcharge` and `total`, so downstream code (invoices, statements, audit logs) can show the components.

For tests I would assert the invariant directly rather than only checking worked examples: for any non-negative amount, `base + surcharge == total`, and the total equals 3% of the amount within the rounding tolerance. I would add cases at zero, a small amount where rounding bites (for example an amount whose 1% falls below half a minor unit), and a large amount, plus a check that the total is never hard-coded independently of the two rates.
