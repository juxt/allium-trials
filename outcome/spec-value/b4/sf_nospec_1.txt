"Add a grace period" sounds like a one-line config change, but three of the decisions underneath it change customer-facing behaviour and can't be defaulted safely. I'll state where I'd land on each, and flag the two that need product sign-off before I merge.

**The decision that must go back to product: waive or defer.** When the grace window expires and the instalment is still unpaid, does the penalty accrue from the *end* of grace, or retroactively from the *original due date*? A borrower who reads "grace period" expects the former: pay within the window and you owe nothing, miss it and the clock starts at day G+1. A collections team often means the latter: the grace is a hold, and breaching it makes the whole period penalisable. The two produce different ledger amounts on every late loan, and the difference compounds for percentage-based penalties. I'd default to **forgive-on-breach** (penalty accrues only from grace expiry) because it matches the plain-language promise in the request, but this is the one line I want product to confirm in writing, because reversing it later means re-pricing live arrears.

**The second decision for product: does grace move days-past-due, or only the money?** The request says "penalised", which is monetary. But delinquency classification, arrears buckets, and anything fed to a credit bureau also key off days-past-due from the due date. If grace shifts DPD, a borrower one day past a seven-day grace shows as one day delinquent, not eight, and that flows into risk reporting and potentially external reporting obligations. My default is to **decouple them**: suppress the penalty during grace but keep DPD and delinquency measured from the true due date, so internal risk still sees reality and only the charge is held. Fineract already separates these (penalty application versus `graceOnArrearsAgeing` for delinquency), so this is also the lower-risk build. But it's a policy call with compliance edges, so product and whoever owns bureau reporting should confirm.

Everything below I'd default and note in the PR rather than block on.

**Where it's configured.** Per loan product, not global and not per-charge. Fineract already ships a global `penalty-wait-period` and a product-level arrears-ageing grace, so before writing anything I'd confirm whether the team actually wants to extend those existing knobs or add a parallel one. My strong preference is to reuse the product-level grace concept and drive penalty timing from it, rather than introduce a third overlapping setting. A per-loan override at disbursement is out of scope unless asked.

**Units and boundary.** Calendar days, configured as an integer, default 0 so existing products are unchanged. The window is inclusive of the grace days and the penalty triggers on day G+1: due date is day 0, a five-day grace means a payment on day 5 is still clean and the penalty first applies on day 6. I'm choosing calendar over business days for predictability; if the team wants weekends and holidays excluded that's a real but separate change to the schedule engine.

**Per instalment.** Grace applies independently to each overdue instalment, not once per loan. Two missed instalments each get their own window from their own due date.

**Concrete behaviour in Fineract terms.** The overdue-penalty batch job (the step that applies `OVERDUE_INSTALLMENT` penalty charges) already walks instalments past due and posts the charge. I'd add the grace test there: an instalment is only eligible for penalty once `today > dueDate + graceDays`, and under forgive-on-breach the accrual/charge date is set to `dueDate + graceDays + 1` rather than the due date, so the posted amount and any per-day accrual start from the correct point. Nothing changes about how the charge posts to the ledger once triggered. Delinquency classification is left on its existing path, measuring from due date, per the decoupling decision above.

**Edges I'd handle.** A payment that clears within grace never triggers a penalty at all, so no post-and-waive churn on the ledger. A backdated payment or a reversal that moves the effective date across the grace boundary re-evaluates eligibility on the next job run. Existing loans in flight: grace defaults to 0, so nothing already accruing changes unless an operator sets a non-zero value, at which point it applies prospectively from the next job run, not retroactively unwinding penalties already posted.

**What I'd ship.** The product-level field with a migration defaulting to 0, the eligibility and charge-date change in the overdue job, and tests covering the boundary (day G clean, day G+1 charged), the forgive-versus-defer amount, multiple instalments, and the reversal-across-boundary case. I'd hold the merge until product confirms forgive-on-breach and the DPD decoupling, since both are cheap in code now and expensive to change once live loans have accrued under them.
