This request is one sentence standing in for about a dozen policy decisions, and the whole point of the v4 loop is that I don't get to pick the answers on the product team's behalf. So before I write any `observable state` or `invariant`, here is what I can settle myself, what I have to ask, and where I expect `allium analyse` to bite.

**What I'll encode as constraints and ask the operator to confirm or correct.** These follow from the brief and standard servicing practice, so surfacing them as open questions would just waste attention. The grace applies to the late-payment penalty only. The word is "penalised", so interest accrual and other due-date-triggered charges keep their current behaviour unless told otherwise. It applies to both the fixed-amount and percentage penalty configurations, since both are "the penalty". It runs per instalment, keyed off each instalment's own due date, so every missed instalment gets its own window rather than one window per loan. And if the instalment is paid in full inside the window, no late-payment penalty arises for it. I'll write each of these as a constraint and let the operator veto it.

**What I will not guess.** These depend on this org's policy or on regulatory posture, and fabricating them is exactly the failure this process exists to prevent. Each becomes an `-- OPEN:` line, and the spec is not finished while any remain:

- Grace length. How many days, and calendar or business days. A threshold I cannot know.
- The retroactivity question, which is the real crux. Once the window lapses and the instalment is still unpaid, does the penalty accrue from the *original due date* (so grace only helps those who pay in time, and latecomers are billed the full run) or only from the *end of the window* (so the first N days are permanently forgiven)? "Aren't penalised the instant they're a day late" is consistent with both readings. It changes the money, and it is not mine to decide.
- Whether the grace also moves delinquency classification. Today days-past-due and all downstream reporting key off the due date. The request talks only about penalties, but the existing system couples the two. Does a borrower inside the window show as current, or as several days past due but not yet charged? That is a reporting and quite possibly a regulatory decision, and it is easy to miss precisely because the change request never mentions it.
- Scope and rollout. All loan products or configurable per product. And for loans already delinquent at cutover, does the new window apply retroactively or only to due dates after go-live.

**Where I expect the checker to bite, and why the encoding matters.** The retroactivity answer and the "no penalty within grace" intent must constrain the *same* state. If I model "penalty accrues from the original due date" and "the borrower is not penalised for days inside the window" as two independent flags, the contradiction slips through the gap between them and `analyse` passes vacuously on a spec that forbids nothing. So I'll model one predicate, the date from which penalty accrues for an instalment, and let both answers land on it. If the operator says accrual runs from the original due date *and* that the in-window days are never charged, those cannot both hold, and I want the minimal conflicting core in front of them rather than smoothed away by me. I'll also assert the guarded case is reachable, an instalment that is paid inside the window and one that lapses past it, so a "penalty must be within grace" rule can't pass only because the penalised case never occurs.

So the next cycle is a single question: of the retroactivity readings above, which one holds? I'll capture the answer, wire it to the accrual-date predicate alongside the delinquency-coupling answer, run `allium analyse`, and only then move on. I'm not writing the spec body until those OPEN lines are answered.
