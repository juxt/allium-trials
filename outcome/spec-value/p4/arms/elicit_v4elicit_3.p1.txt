## The one decision that defines the product

**1. Interest method: amortising (reducing balance) or flat rate?**

Both give equal monthly instalments, so the brief does not settle this.

- *Amortising / annuity:* interest each month is charged on the outstanding balance. `instalment = P·r / (1 − (1+r)^−n)`. Early instalments are mostly interest, later ones mostly principal.
- *Flat rate:* interest is `P · annual_rate · years` on the original principal, added up front, then `(P + interest) / n`. Every month's interest portion is identical.

For a £10,000 loan the total interest differs by a large factor, and the per-row interest/principal split that `schedule` returns is entirely different. I recommend amortising as the consumer-lending default, but I am flagging it rather than encoding it silently. Which is it?

## Defaults I am proposing — confirm or correct

These are settled enough by standard practice that I will encode them unless you say otherwise:

- **Payments in arrears.** First instalment falls one month after disbursement (ordinary annuity, not annuity due).
- **Monthly rate = `annual_rate_pct / 100 / 12`** (nominal, simple division). Not the effective `(1+a)^(1/12)−1`. Say if your `annual_rate_pct` is meant to be an effective APR.
- **Zero-rate case:** when `annual_rate_pct = 0`, `instalment = disbursed / months`. The annuity formula divides by zero here, so this is a real branch.
- **Input domain:** `disbursed > 0`, `months` a positive integer, `annual_rate_pct >= 0`.
