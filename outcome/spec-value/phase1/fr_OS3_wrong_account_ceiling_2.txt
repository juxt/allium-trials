The double-entry spec is satisfied. Both legs still post `amount`, so debits equal credits, and the maintained property holds. If spec conformance were the only bar, this passes.

But spec conformance is not the same as safe. The change routes penalty income into `incomeFee`, collapsing two distinct revenue accounts into one. The ledger can no longer distinguish penalty income from fee income. That distinction is not cosmetic: penalty and fee revenue are separate categories for financial reporting, regulatory disclosure, and often tax treatment. Balancing entries into the wrong account is a classic misclassification defect: the books balance to the penny and still misstate what the money is.

The maintained spec is silent on account identity. It constrains the *sum* of legs, not *which* accounts they hit. That silence is exactly the blind spot here. "Compiles, tests pass, debits equal credits" is necessary but not sufficient, and the comment `// Debit and credit still equal` is arguing to the wrong invariant. The right check is whether penalty postings land in a penalty account, which the spec never asserts and these tests evidently don't either.

This is a case where the relational property survives intact while a semantic bug walks straight through it. The spec gives no cover to merge.

DECISION: BLOCK
REASON: The change misclassifies penalty income as fee income, a real accounting defect that the double-entry balance spec cannot see because it constrains only the equality of legs, not which accounts they post to.
