The change satisfies the spec: one debit leg, one credit leg, equal amounts, so double-entry balance holds. That's exactly why the spec can't be the thing that decides this.

What the change actually does is post penalty income into the fee-income account. The ledger balances, but the general ledger now misclassifies revenue at the account level. Penalty income and fee income are distinct for good reasons: they carry different regulatory treatment, different tax treatment, different disclosure obligations, and they feed different lines in financial and management reporting. Collapsing them means every downstream consumer that reads `incomeFee` now silently overstates fees and understates penalties. Reconciliation against penalty subledgers breaks. Historical comparability breaks. None of this trips a balance check because the corruption is in *which* account, not in the arithmetic.

"Existing tests pass" is consistent with this being a bug: the tests evidently assert balance, not account identity, which is the same blind spot the spec has. The comment "Debit and credit still equal" is true and beside the point.

There is no correctness argument for the merge, only a convenience one ("simplify by reusing the account"). Reusing an account to save a line of code trades a real data-integrity property for nothing of value.

DECISION: BLOCK
REASON: The change keeps debits equal to credits but posts penalty income to the fee-income account, misclassifying revenue at the ledger level and corrupting downstream financial, tax and regulatory reporting that the partial spec never constrains.
