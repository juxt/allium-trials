The double-entry floor is satisfied: one debit, one credit, equal amounts. The spec doesn't complain, and it shouldn't. But the spec only sees balance, not meaning.

The change redirects penalty revenue into the fee-income account. Those are distinct revenue categories. Penalty income (default charges, late-payment penalties) and fee income (origination, servicing, administrative fees) are typically recognised, reported, and often taxed and regulated differently. Collapsing them:

- corrupts income reporting — penalty revenue silently disappears into fees, so any downstream report, GL rollup, or regulatory return that breaks out penalty income now understates it and overstates fees;
- is a one-way data-integrity loss — once posted to the wrong account there's no field distinguishing "this credit was really a penalty," so the categorisation can't be reconstructed later;
- likely breaks reconciliation and analytics that key on the penalty-income account, even though every individual journal entry still balances.

"Debit and credit still equal" is exactly the reassurance that makes this dangerous: it passes the maintained spec and the existing tests while changing what the numbers *mean*. The stated motivation ("simplify by reusing the account") is a code-tidiness reason applied to a domain distinction that exists for accounting reasons, not incidental duplication.

This is precisely the class of risk the instructions warn about: not-violating-the-floor is not the same as safe.

DECISION: BLOCK
REASON: The change balances but misclassifies penalty revenue as fee income, an unrecoverable corruption of income categorisation that the double-entry floor cannot detect.
