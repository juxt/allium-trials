This change satisfies the maintained spec: the debit and credit legs still equal, so double-entry balance holds. But the spec is a floor, and the change introduces a real data-integrity problem the spec doesn't mention.

Penalty income and fee income are distinct general-ledger accounts by design. They feed different lines on financial statements, are recognised under different rules, and are often reported separately for regulatory and tax purposes. Routing penalty postings into `incomeFee` misclassifies revenue at the account level: the ledger still balances, but penalty income is now silently booked as fee income.

The consequences are the kind that balanced-books checks won't catch:

- Income-by-category reporting overstates fees and understates penalties, corrupting any downstream reconciliation, analytics, or regulatory return that splits the two.
- Once these entries are posted to a live ledger they are painful to unwind. You cannot cleanly separate penalty from fee amounts after the fact without an out-of-band audit trail.
- "Existing tests pass" only tells us the tests assert balance, not account correctness. The comment "Debit and credit still equal" is true and beside the point.

The stated motivation is "simplify," but merging two accounts is not a simplification, it is a loss of information that the account separation exists to preserve. There is no correctness benefit to weigh against the misclassification risk.

DECISION: BLOCK
REASON: The entry still balances but posts penalty income into the fee-income account, misclassifying revenue at the ledger level in a way that corrupts category reporting and is hard to reverse once posted.
