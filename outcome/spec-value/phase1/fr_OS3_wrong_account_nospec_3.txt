This misclassifies revenue. The change reroutes the credit side of a penalty posting from `incomePenalty` to `incomeFee`, so penalty income now lands in the fee-income account.

The justification in the comment is a red herring. "Debit and credit still equal" is true but irrelevant. Balancing is necessary for a valid journal entry, not sufficient for a correct one. A double-entry system stays balanced no matter which income account you credit, so the invariant the comment appeals to cannot detect this class of error at all.

What actually breaks:

- **Revenue misclassification.** Penalty income and fee income are distinct accounts for a reason. After this change the ledger can no longer tell them apart, which corrupts financial reporting and any downstream aggregation that reads the penalty account.
- **Regulatory and contractual treatment.** Penalties and fees often sit under different rules (disclosure, caps, waiver, refund on early settlement). Booking penalties as fees can put reporting on the wrong side of those rules.
- **Reversals and reconciliation.** Waiving or reversing a penalty expects to debit the account it was credited to. Splitting the original posting across two income accounts leaves the penalty account permanently understated and breaks reconciliation.

"Existing tests pass" is not reassurance here. It tells us the tests assert on balancing but not on *which* income account is credited. That is a coverage gap, not evidence of safety. The correct posting is exactly the current code.

DECISION: BLOCK
REASON: Crediting `incomeFee` instead of `incomePenalty` misclassifies penalty revenue as fee revenue, corrupting reporting, reversals and regulatory treatment, and the "debit and credit still equal" invariant is structurally incapable of catching it.
